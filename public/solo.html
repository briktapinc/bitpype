01

The model: why solo works now

A traditional agency is mostly coordination cost — designers waiting on developers, account managers relaying messages, somebody manually checking forty client sites. BitPype collapses the production side into one platform, which changes what "an agency" has to be.

The old way

A team for everything

Sales rep, designer, developer, copywriter, account manager, bookkeeper. Every client payment feeds five salaries before it feeds you — so you need big clients and big retainers just to stand still.

The BitPype way

A platform for production

Lead discovery, site design and publishing, call tracking, forms and lead capture, bookings, follow-up emails, even a client portal — one system produces and operates the sites. Your hours go where humans actually matter.

Your job

Judgment & relationships

You pick the right businesses, approve what goes out, talk to owners like a person, and keep promises. That's the part clients pay for and remember. Everything repetitive is the platform's problem.

The solo equation

Recurring monthly clients × a price a local business barely notices = an income no single client can take away from you.

Solo doesn't mean small. It means diversified: thirty businesses paying you monthly is sturdier than three big ones — lose one and you've lost a bad afternoon, not your rent.

What you sell

Offer 1 · Core

Website + Care Plan

A custom site, built for that one business, hosted and maintained, with call tracking and lead capture built in. Sold as a monthly plan — the site is never "finished and forgotten," it's staffed.

  • Design, build, publish, done-for-you changes

Offer 2 · Growth

The Module Stack

Once the core is live, sell the working parts: online bookings, instant estimates, a chatbot that answers at 2 a.m., Google reviews on-site, memberships, a shop. Each module is a reason the monthly number grows.

  • Attach one module at a time, prove it, add the next
  • Every module runs inside the same portal

Offer 3 · Landlord

Rank & Rent

Build a lead-generation site in a niche you pick, rank it in one town, then rent the calls and leads to the best local operator in that trade. One exclusive tenant per niche per town.

  • You own the asset; they pay for results
  • Charges per qualified call or lead

⚠ Pick niches like a landlord, not a fan

A rent niche must pass two tests at once: weak competition (few or poor existing sites) and strong search demand (real people searching every month). A niche that's only quiet is quiet for a reason; a niche that's only popular is a knife fight. Both tests, or walk away.

03

The pipeline, stage by stage

Every client travels the same five stages, in this order. The order is the product — it's what makes a one-person operation feel like a firm.

1FindSpot the business, open the lead
2BuildDesign staged, unique to them
3ApproveHuman eyes before anything moves
4PublishLive, domain pointed, verified
5SupportChanges, calls, leads, rent
  • Find: one lead record per business, one email per lead — the platform enforces it, so your list never forks into duplicates.
  • Verify before you build: real services, real hours, real photos and branding from their own pages and listings. Never invent a claim — no made-up testimonials, hours, prices, or certifications. If you can't verify it, it doesn't go on the site.
  • Build staged: the site exists, fully designed, where only you can see it. Generous padding, text never touching container edges, a design that couldn't be mistaken for your last client's.
  • Approve: nothing publishes and no outreach message sends before approval. This single rule prevents nearly every expensive mistake a solo operator can make.
  • Publish & prove: link the domain, let the platform create the DNS record, then open the real address and see the site answer. "Set up" is not the check — loading it is.
  • Support: the client's changes come through their portal as change requests. Work the queue like a ticket desk: read, do, confirm.

05

Finding clients

Your ideal client is a good business that's invisible online: busy, well-reviewed, and running on a Facebook page and a prayer. BitPype's discovery finds them; your judgment picks the winners.

Qualify IN

The right signs

  • Independent — no franchise or chain
  • Real demand: reviews, a busy shop, years in business
  • No working website, or one that embarrasses them
  • A phone number you can actually reach
  • A trade where one job pays for a year of you (roofing, HVAC, dumpsters, dentists…)
Qualify OUT

The time-wasters

  • Chains and franchises — decisions happen elsewhere
  • No phone, no reviews, no footprint — no business yet
  • Already has a modern site with booking that works
  • Wants a quote "for my nephew to compare" — you're the homework, not the hire

Work a territory, not the whole internet. One county, a handful of trades, a route you could drive. Local compounds: every client you land in a town makes the next one in that town easier — owners talk, and your sites become your showroom.

Pricing without a boss

Nobody approves your prices, which means nobody saves you from bad ones. These principles keep a solo operator profitable without gouging.

Charge monthly, think yearly

A monthly plan priced like a utility bill is an easy yes and a hard cancel — the site, hosting, call tracking, changes, and care are visibly working every month. Anchor the conversation on what one booked job is worth to them; for most trades, your month costs less than one hour of one job.

Offer three rungs

An entry plan, a core plan most people pick, and a premium plan with the module stack and priority care. Three rungs turn "should I?" into "which one?" — and the middle rung is where your business lives. Whatever numbers you choose, set them once, write them down, and stop renegotiating them per client.

Respect the setup question

A setup fee filters for serious buyers and pays for your build week; waiving it on annual plans rewards commitment. Either model works — mixing them ad hoc doesn't. Pick your policy before your first pitch, not during it.

Never discount the retainer

Discounting recurring revenue discounts every future month too. If a price objection is real, shrink the scope (fewer modules, slower changes), never the number. A smaller plan at full price beats a full plan at a resentful one.

⚠ Test money is not money

Before you tell anyone you can take their payment, confirm the platform is in live mode — test checkouts feel identical and charge nothing. And when a checkout fails with "no such price," the price almost certainly lives in the wrong account or the wrong mode. Check that before you promise a fix.

07

Running clients day to day

Clients don't churn because a page loads slowly. They churn because they feel forgotten. The portal is your retention machine — everything below is already in it.

  • Give every client their portal login on day one. When a client can see their calls, their form leads, and their site's activity, they see your value without you writing a report.
  • Work the Changes tab like a promise. Every change request gets a reply the same business day — even if the reply is "done Thursday." Silence is the only unforgivable response time.
  • Glance at LeadScout weekly per client. If submissions aren't turning into contacts, something's misconfigured — find it before the client notices a missing lead.
  • Protect the phone path. The site's number is a tracking number; the client's real phone is only the forwarding destination. Set it, then test-call it. A typo here fails silently — in missing customers.
  • Keep domains boring. Subscribed clients renew automatically; anyone else is manual-renewal. Before you reassure a client about their domain, open it and read which mode it's in.
  • Send one useful thing a month. A short newsletter from your mailing list, or a note: "Your site took 14 tracked calls last month." Proof of work, in their inbox, beats a holiday card.

Money operations

Solo means there's no finance department to catch your mistakes, so the routine has to catch them.

Weekly (15 minutes, Friday)

  • Finance page: what came in, what the platform spent
  • Any failed payments or expiring domains — act while they're small
  • Rental charges: exactly once per qualified event, never twice

Monthly (one hour)

  • Reconcile subscriptions against live clients — no ghost charges, no free riders
  • Review plan mix: who's outgrown their rung? That's your upsell list
  • Check the mailing list send log: sent, failed, unsubscribed — real numbers only

Remember the platform's memory: the Audit log records every consequential action — clears, settings changes, charges. Work like it's watching, because it is, and that's what lets you trust your own operation when a client asks "what happened here?"

11

Solo guardrails

The risks of solo aren't competition — they're overcommitment and quiet mistakes. These rules keep one person safe.

  • Capacity is real. Three quality builds a week is a pace you can hold for years. Six is a month you can hold once, followed by churn. Protect the pace.
  • Creating is free; publishing, sending, charging, and destroying need a pause. Every irreversible action gets a breath and a second look at its confirmation — especially lead deletes, which take everything attached with them.
  • Never improvise what the platform provides. No hand-built forms, no side spreadsheets for leads, no card numbers in chat. The system's way is the auditable way.
  • Honor the suppression list absolutely. One message to someone who opted out costs more trust than a hundred good sends earn.
  • When something looks wrong, stop first. The Dashboard's Emergency stop exists for you. Halting automation is always allowed; explaining why you didn't is the hard conversation.
  • Keep secrets like cash. Tokens, passwords, and platform backups contain live credentials. Your two-factor stays on, your recovery codes stay saved, and none of it ever goes in a chat message.
