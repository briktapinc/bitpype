02

What we need from you

Gather these once, at the start, and you'll rarely be asked for anything again. Items marked Needed hold up the build without them; Helpful items make your site stronger but won't block it.

  • Your logo — the best file you have (the one from your sign shop or printer is perfect). NeededWhy: it anchors your site's colors and identity. No logo? Tell us — we'll work from your signage and truck lettering instead.
  • Your exact business hours — including holidays and seasonal changes. NeededWhy: wrong hours are the #1 reason customers get frustrated — and Google shows them everywhere.
  • Your services, in your words — the full list of what you actually do, and what you want more of. NeededWhy: we only publish services you really offer. If it's not on your list, it won't be on your site.
  • The phone number that should ring — the one you actually answer during business hours. NeededWhy: your website displays its own tracking number, and every call forwards to this one. Double-check the digits — a typo here means missed customers, quietly.
  • Your service area — the towns and neighborhoods you want work from. NeededWhy: this is how your site gets found by people near you — and how we avoid sending you jobs an hour away.
  • Your domain choice — pick your web address from the options we send, or tell us about one you already own. NeededWhy: nothing goes live until your name points at your site. While your plan stays active, your domain renews automatically — one less thing to remember.
  • Photos of your real work — your crew, your truck, finished jobs, your shop. Phone photos are great. HelpfulWhy: real photos outsell stock photos every time. Customers hire the business they can picture showing up.
  • Your Google listing — if you have a Google Business Profile, tell us which email it's under. HelpfulWhy: we can connect it to your portal so your hours and info stay in sync — and your real Google reviews can appear on your site.
  • One decision-maker — the person who approves the design and requests changes. HelpfulWhy: one voice keeps your site consistent. Everyone else can still view the portal — approvals just move faster with one name on them.

04

Go-live day — your two checks

We test everything before we call a site live. Then we ask you to do the two checks only you can do:

  • Open your address and look at your site. Not the preview link — your real domain, on your phone, like a customer. If it loads and looks like you, we're live.
  • Call your website's number from another phone. Your phone should ring. That one call proves the whole chain — the number on the site, the forwarding, and the call log in your portal, where you'll see the test call listed.

A note about Google

Getting found on Google is a climb, not a switch. Your site launches with all the right foundations — sitemap, business details, local pages — but rankings build over weeks as Google learns to trust the site. Anyone who promises you page one overnight is selling you something. We'll show you your analytics honestly as they grow.

05

Your first 30 days

A website pays off fastest when its owner does four small things. None of them takes more than ten minutes.

Weekly

Glance at your portal

Check your calls and form leads once a week. You'll spot patterns fast — which services people ask about, which hours they call. That's marketing intelligence you used to pay for, or guess at.

After every good job

Ask for the review

Happy customer in front of you? Ask them to leave a Google review and tell them your website makes it easy to find you. Reviews are the fuel your rankings run on.

Whenever something changes

Use the Changes tab

New service, new hire, holiday hours, a price you're proud of — send it through Changes the day it happens. A website that's always current is one customers trust with a booking.

Once

Put your address everywhere

Your trucks, invoices, business cards, Facebook page, email signature. Every place your name appears is a chance for someone to land on a site that books the appointment for you.

06

Getting help

One rule covers almost everything: if it's about your website, it starts in the Changes tab of your portal. Your request lands with our team, attached to your site, with nothing lost in a text thread.

A great change request looks like…

"Update our hours — we now close at 4 on Fridays." One request per change, in your own words. Attach the photo right to the request when it's about a photo. You'll hear back the same business day, even if the work itself lands a day later.

If something seems broken…

Note what you saw and when, and send it the same way. "The booking form didn't send at 2pm Tuesday" is a report we can act on immediately. Screenshots help if you have one — if not, your words are enough.
